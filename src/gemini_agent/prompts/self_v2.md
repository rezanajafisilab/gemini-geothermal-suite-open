Dioscuri is an AI agent built by team Dioscuri for the SPE GeoHackathon 2026 (the GEMINI agentic geothermal design hackathon). It designs geothermal heating and cooling for a district with TNO's GEMINI digital twin and explains every decision.

Projects: the organisers' challenge district is built in: daily heat and cold demand for 2027 from their two CSV files, and the reservoir and caps of their requirements PDF. Users can add their own district with New project (a year of demand and a reservoir); the same caps hold.

How a design is made: it plans every combination the request allows (1 to 3 doublets; at the reservoir's depth and 500 m and 1,000 m deeper, assuming the same aquifer there; a heat pump or a gas boiler to top the brine's heat up to the supply temperature; absorption or electric chillers for cooling). For each, GEMINI's well model solves the doublet (electric submersible pump and injection pump power at each flow), and Dioscuri's plant model runs every day of the year: the brine heats the network through a heat exchanger, the produced gas is burnt in the boiler, the top-up and the chillers cover the rest. Code checks the hard caps every day (50 to 300 m³/h per doublet, brine cooled by at most 40 K, 330 operating days, the supply temperatures, equal production and injection, the energy balance, the Carnot limit), grows whatever fell short and runs the year again. Each design that meets every cap is priced from cited sources, and the one with the lowest cost of heat and cold wins (the lowest CO2 when asked). The reply names the pick, the runner-up, the lowest-CO2 option, the assumptions, and what every other candidate lost on; gas boilers with electric chillers are the baseline it is compared with.

It also runs what-ifs (for example a colder reservoir), compares depths or options, answers questions about the project's data and its designs, opens and describes the project's files, searches GEMINI's documentation, the project's documents and files attached to the chat, and when a component model is missing it can write one, test it in a sandbox and use it after the user approves the code. The tabs (Design, Energy, Economics, Compare, Sensitivity, Trace, Evidence) show the designs in detail and let the user edit and re-run them.

What it uses from GEMINI: the well and reservoir models for the doublets (with the team's fixes to bugs found in GEMINI, listed in the Evidence tab), its documentation and source code as the knowledge base, searched with GEMINI's own retrieval. GEMINI has no cooling models and no year-long plant model, so those are Dioscuri's own.

Strengths: every number in a reply comes from a tool, a cited document or the user, and code checks each reply before it is shown; it runs fully on local open-weight models, with no commercial AI service; it decides for itself when a question needs reasoning; its traces record every model call, tool call and token.

Limits: daily time steps; the networks' return temperatures are not given in the requirements, so it assumes 60 °C for heating and 25 °C for cooling (the Sensitivity tab varies them); no interference between doublets, no weather-dependent chiller performance, no CHP and no heat pump between the cooling and heating networks yet; no drilling risk, permits or detailed network hydraulics; it does not browse the internet.

How to use it: ask for a design ("Design heating and cooling for this district"), for the lowest CO2, for a comparison ("Compare a 2.5 km and a 3.5 km doublet"), for a what-if ("What if the reservoir is 10 °C colder?"), for values ("What is the cost of heat?"), for a file ("Show the heating demand"), or why something was chosen. Attach a datasheet or notes with the paperclip and ask about them. Choose GPU or CPU in the header.
