Dioscuri is an AI agent built by team Dioscuri for the SPE GeoHackathon 2026 (the GEMINI agentic geothermal design hackathon). It designs geothermal heating and cooling for a district with TNO's GEMINI digital twin and explains every decision.

Projects: the organisers' challenge district is built in: daily heat and cold demand for 2027 from their two CSV files, and the reservoir and caps of their requirements PDF. Users can add their own district with New project (a year of demand and a reservoir); the same caps hold.

How a design is made: before the first design of a chat it shows what it read from the project (demand, reservoir, brine, caps) and what it assumes (network returns, prices, the heat capacity of the brine), and runs once the user confirms. It then tries every plant layout the request allows: 1 to 3 doublets in the known aquifer (a deeper, assumed aquifer only when asked, since the requirements describe one aquifer at 2,500 m), a heat pump or a boiler to top the brine's heat up to the supply temperature, absorption or electric chillers, and two or more doublets sharing the brine equally or the hottest first. Each layout is sized many ways (heat pump and absorption chiller sizes); each sizing runs every day of the year: GEMINI's well model gives each doublet's pumps at its flow, and Dioscuri's plant model runs the heat exchanger, the produced gas in the boiler, the top-up and the chillers. Code checks the hard caps every day (50 to 300 m³/h per doublet, brine cooled by at most 40 K, 330 operating days, the supply temperatures, equal production and injection, the energy balance, the Carnot limit), grows whatever fell short and runs the year again. Then it tries variants of the best three: two injectors per doublet (one producer, two injectors), a treatment train (separator and filters) per doublet, and wells open over only half of the 100 m aquifer. Code also checks the subsurface: when the cold water from the injector reaches the producer (Gringarten-Sauty, against the 30-year life), whether the ESP's intake stays above the gas bubble point (about 43 bar, with a 5 bar margin at the ESP's intake), and SodM's injection-pressure limit (0.135 bar per metre); a doublet that breaks one runs at the highest flow within them. The cheapest layouts are also sized as reliable plans: the boiler and chillers in equal units, grown until any one unit can be out (a doublet, its ESP, a treatment train, the heat exchanger, the heat pump, a boiler or chiller unit) and every day is still met. Each design that meets every cap is priced from cited sources (ThermoGIS v3 and PBL's SDE++ 2026 for geothermal). It also sizes gas-free designs, which buy no gas: heat pumps carry the heat and an electric boiler the peaks and the backup, while the brine's own gas is either burnt in a small boiler or cleaned and sold to the gas grid (then the buyer's CO2 from burning it is said apart, not counted as the plant's). Six picks come out: the cheapest (lowest cost of heat and cold), the lowest CO2, the cheapest reliable plan, the greenest reliable plan, the gas-free one (the cheapest reliable plan with no gas bought) and the best overall, which it recommends: a reliable plan that is the cheapest when CO2 counts at the long-term price of 137 €/t. A user may ask for any of them instead. The recommended design's brine loop is also run on GEMINI itself and set against the plant model (pressures, temperatures, pump powers, exchanger duty) as a check. Two options on the inputs card, both off unless the user switches them on: the district network's heat losses (one distance to the district; the plant makes the loss and supplies a little hotter so the buildings still get 100 °C), and each unit's yearly service (each unit out once a year in its quietest weeks). A request may also fix how the doublets are built, each one apart (its aquifer, its open parts, one or two injectors, its own treatment train). Designs are named A1, A2 … (the first search, ranked), B1 … (the next), W1 … (what-ifs), U1 … (the user's), with the layout code beside the name. The reply names the pick, the other picks, what its variants cost more and the assumptions; gas boilers with electric chillers are the baseline.

It also runs what-ifs on any input it has (a network return temperature, the reservoir's temperature, permeability or thickness, the caps, the demand, prices, a unit's size, how the doublets are built): the same plant under the change, and the search again to see whether another design would now win; it says plainly when something asked for is not in its model. It runs failures: a unit out on given days ("the ESP of doublet 1 fails from 10 to 24 January"), for a number of days (the worst start in the year is found) or all year (one unit out), says what is not delivered, and what would help through the same days: more boiler, more chillers, a standby unit or a spare ESP on the shelf, with their cost; and what failures cost a design over a year on average (from each unit's failure rate and repair time, with sources) and whether a spare ESP pays. It sets designs side by side by code, every figure from the designs' runs, and takes requests of up to four steps ("design it, then check it 10 °C colder, and say if the pick changes"), shown as a plan that ticks off each step; it can end with a decision table (cost per home, against gas, CO2, gas share, one unit out) or the uncertainties that matter most. Besides the plant's cost (what society pays, which ranks the designs), the Economics tab and the chat give an investor's view: heat and cold sold at the gas alternative's cost, SDE++ 2026 by the plant's category, corporate tax, and the NPV, IRR and payback after tax, with and without the subsidy. The Compare tab lists every design of the chat (each search's designs, what-ifs and the user's), filters, sorts and sets up to four side by side; a design's report for operators downloads as a PDF. It compares options, answers questions about the project's data and its designs, opens and describes the project's files, searches GEMINI's documentation, the project's documents and files attached to the chat, and when a component model is missing it can write one, test it in a sandbox and use it after the user approves the code. The tabs (Design, Energy, Economics, Compare, Sensitivity, Trace, Evidence) show the designs in detail and let the user edit and re-run them.

What it uses from GEMINI: the well and reservoir models for the doublets (with the team's fixes to bugs found in GEMINI, listed in the Evidence tab), its documentation and source code as the knowledge base, searched with GEMINI's own retrieval. GEMINI has no cooling models and no year-long plant model, so those are Dioscuri's own.

Strengths: every number in a reply comes from a tool, a cited document or the user, and code checks each reply before it is shown; it runs fully on local open-weight models, with no commercial AI service; it decides for itself when a question needs reasoning; its traces record every model call, tool call and token.

Limits: daily time steps; the networks' return temperatures are not given in the requirements, so it assumes 60 °C for heating and 25 °C for cooling (the Sensitivity tab varies them); no reservoir simulation, so breakthrough is an analytical estimate per doublet and there is no interference between doublets or sinking of the cold brine (which part of the aquifer an injector is open to changes only its injectivity in the model); a spare treatment train is costed but its value when one is serviced is not; no weather-dependent chiller performance, no CHP and no heat pump between the cooling and heating networks yet; no drilling risk, permits or detailed network hydraulics (the network's losses are a rule of thumb for one pipe pair); it does not browse the internet.

How to use it: ask for a design ("Design heating and cooling for this district"), for the lowest CO2 or the cheapest, for a failure ("What if the heat pump fails for two weeks?"), for a build ("two doublets, the second with its own separator"), for a comparison ("Compare one and two doublets"), for a what-if ("What if the heating return were 50 °C?", "What if gas cost 45 €/MWh?"), for values ("What is the cost of heat?"), for a file ("Show the heating demand"), or why something was chosen. Attach a datasheet or notes with the paperclip and ask about them. Choose GPU or CPU in the header.
